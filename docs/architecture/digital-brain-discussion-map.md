# Digital brain discussion map

**Status:** working discussion framework  
**Version:** 1.12  
**Last Updated:** 2026-10-28

## Purpose

Keep the current conceptual discussion in view while brainstorming individual aspects, including aspects already discussed and those still to explore.

## Summary

This working agenda groups functions separately from concerns shared across them. It links to the accepted [foundational principles](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md) without treating every topic as decided. The row IDs provide references for discussion; their order sets neither priorities nor an implementation sequence.

## How this map relates to the three functional domains

The accepted distinction is between the source and three functional domains: minimal registration, explicit descriptions and statements, and computational representations and comparisons. They are functions, not prescribed data types or mandatory pipeline stages. See [P05](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p05-source-and-three-functional-domains).

The owner's functional names are metadata, labels, and mathematical or computational relationships. They clarify the same domains without introducing a fixed property catalog or a fourth domain. Produced context is itself stored as an item and can receive descriptions in all three domains. See [P25](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p25-controlled-joint-retrieval-and-traceable-results) and [P26](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p26-produced-context-becomes-a-stored-source).

The agenda below covers activities that use those functions, followed by shared concerns that apply across activities. These agenda entries are neither additional layers nor semantic domains.

## Recorded refinement: connection, intake and scope

Connection describes possible information exchange; intake describes initiative, timing and progress through connections; selectivity defines that intake's scope. Authorization is a separate shared concern. F13 and F14 make these distinctions explicit while retaining the existing function IDs.

Selectivity asks whether information belongs to an intake, not whether it is valuable. A criterion can involve content; its purpose determines the distinction. Membership can be within scope, outside scope or undetermined. Unknown membership must not silently become exclusion. Scope changes retain history: widening leaves a future-only versus historical-backfill decision open, while narrowing does not destroy preserved data. Completeness claims refer to the recorded scope, not all external information.

These accepted refinements are recorded in [P17](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p17-connection-is-distinct-from-intake-and-authorization), [P18](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p18-explicit-intake-behavior-and-observable-completeness) and [P19](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p19-selectivity-defines-intake-scope-rather-than-value). Exact criteria, handling undetermined cases, operational intake mechanisms and outbound behavior remain open. No live intake, new source access or technical design is authorized by this update.

## Recorded refinement: identity, packages and recognition

Item identity, a recorded state or version, and its exemplars or representations answer different questions. Equality, copying, derivation and succession require stated evidence; identical content alone does not prove shared identity. Corrections and branching history remain traceable. See [P20](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p20-identity-state-and-exemplar-are-distinct).

A transfer package and its identifier need not identify the enclosed source item or its version, and no one-to-one correspondence is assumed. F15 makes package context explicit alongside F04 identity and F13 connection. See [P21](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p21-transfer-package-source-item-and-version-are-distinct).

Versioned recognition contracts give meaning to source identifiers, timestamps, change signals, repetition and completion claims. Known, unchanged and fully captured remain separate findings. The [contract questions and daily inbox refresh illustration](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p22-explicit-contracts-justify-recognition) connect the principles to repeated intake without selecting a source protocol or implementation.

Source-specific evidence, identifier authority and validity, reset or conflict handling, package contents, completion guarantees and operational responses to uncertainty remain open. This update does not authorize live intake or adopt a transport standard.

## Recorded refinement: description contracts and validity

F05 concerns generic description contracts across all three existing domains, rather than a catalog of individual labels or named metadata. Contracts distinguish function, target, form, interpretation limits, applicability, justified processing, dependencies and revision consequences. Descriptions retain their governing contract versions; reassessment under a revised contract is separately recognizable. See [P23](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p23-description-contracts-span-the-three-functional-domains).

Recording time does not establish validity. Different descriptions or contract versions may coexist, supplement each other or conflict. Replacement, restriction or withdrawal needs a traceable basis, scope and effective conditions. Contract conformance, contextual applicability and assessed truth remain distinct; choosing one description for a use does not invalidate alternatives. See [P24](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p24-validity-is-explicit-and-distinct-from-chronology).

Concrete description types, contract definitions, comparability requirements and validity or replacement rules remain open. No additional semantic domain is introduced.

## Recorded refinement: retrieval, context and label origin

The three functional domains are described as metadata, labels, and mathematical or computational relationships. F06 first concerns controlled retrieval and joint selection through shared descriptions; such selection does not by itself establish contextual meaning or truth. F08 records the actual sources, descriptive identities and versions, contracts, applicability, method, scope and result used, so a historical query differs from a current execution. See [P25](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p25-controlled-joint-retrieval-and-traceable-results).

Contextualization remains a processing responsibility. Its produced context, in any form, is itself stored with identity, descriptions and version history and can become a source for further work. Derived origin remains visible. Context can yield candidates for existing or new labels; candidate assessment contracts remain open. See [P26](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p26-produced-context-becomes-a-stored-source).

A newly stored label carries its own origin, including the originating context version where applicable. Reusing an existing label does not change that origin. Assignment connects a label to a source or context; it is not extra metadata of the label itself. Labeling both sources and context supports their joint selection. No separate rationale document, exact passage reference or complete inference chain is universally required for each assignment. See [P27](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p27-label-origin-is-distinct-from-assignment).

Withdrawal, restriction or correction changes a label assignment's applicability or assessment while retaining its source connection and recorded use in earlier analysis. Current-applicability retrieval and historical-use retrieval remain distinct. Dependent analyses may need reconsideration without becoming automatically invalid. Actual destruction remains the owner's explicit choice. See [P28](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p28-label-applicability-changes-without-erasing-history).

These refinements add neither a semantic domain nor a storage format. Concrete retrieval guarantees, label origin and assignment contracts, candidate assessment rules and contextualization methods remain open.

## Recorded refinement: retrieval responses and access revocation

Retrieval first identifies a desired source, version or source set by a described method and conditions. A response contract separately defines returned content and descriptions; no requirement to return all three descriptive domains is assumed. Historical retrieval traceability remains required. Export and exchange still need their own discussion. See [P29](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p29-retrieval-method-and-response-contract-are-distinct).

The owner is the sole internal user; sources, descriptions and context are private by default. Global owner access comes first. Later, bounded routes may exercise owner-delegated authority for specified information selections and operations without granting independent internal entity entitlements or transferring ownership. Export, sending and use of an external model or service are disclosure boundaries even when that service is the owner's interface. Existing authorization can cover routine operations; this neither requires a new confirmation every time nor permits blanket autonomous publication. See [P39](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p39-exclusive-personal-ownership-precedes-bounded-disclosure).

Control is enforced on access, independently of connection capability. Revocation of granted authority acts atomically within its declared scope and stops further disclosure from its effective point, including unreleased information in running requests. Retention remains distinct and delivered information cannot be recalled. Concrete bounded-route and effective/release contracts remain open. See [P30](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p30-access-accompanies-stored-information-and-is-revocable).

Per-entity multi-party rights are not a planned later phase or target architecture; changing that direction requires explicit owner reconsideration. Internal least-privilege and security boundaries remain legitimate protection. The private-thoughts analogy is a confidentiality requirement, not proof of absolute secrecy.

## Historical non-decision: row-level security

Earlier discussion considered PostgreSQL Row-Level Security without adopting it. P39 now refines the significance of that exploration: it is not a planned route toward third-party rights per internal entity. No database or enforcement technology is selected or prohibited by this clarification.

## Recorded refinement: completeness and usability

Exactness does not establish completeness. Intake completeness concerns receiving and storing everything in the agreed intake scope; processing completeness concerns the intended operation on all involved inputs; retrieval completeness concerns everything deliverable under its method, conditions and access rights. Completeness is assessed against a described scope and operation, never all possible knowledge. Complete, incomplete and unknown completeness are distinct judgments; unverified scope or coverage remains unknown. Successful operations and intact captured items establish only what was actually checked. An empty result alone does not prove nonexistence. See [P31](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p31-completeness-is-relative-to-scope-and-operation).

Partial results may be supplied with recognizable actual execution, assessed completeness, known limits and unknowns. The system must not silently present them as complete or promise an inventory of unknown missing items. Reporting remains within access rights and must not reveal inaccessible existence. The applicable contract or recipient within their authority may assess sufficiency for a purpose; mandatory constraints still apply. Incomplete can be usable, while usability does not make a result complete. See [P32](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p32-partial-results-and-usability-are-distinct-from-completeness).

This records a shared principle for F01, F08, F09, F11 and F12 without completing their design. Coverage evidence, thresholds, stop or continuation rules, recovery and presentation remain open.

## Recorded refinement: uncertainty belongs to the source application

A reusable label or description retains its own registration, definition, identity, origin and versioned meaning. Its application connects it to a source or source version. Uncertainty or dispute about that fit belongs to the application: a clear definition can be applied uncertainly. See [P33](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p33-application-uncertainty-is-distinct-from-definition).

Source statements remain distinct from assessments. Preserve conflicting concrete applications or statements without silently declaring one true. A reasoned choice for a use does not erase alternatives or automatically invalidate them. An application dispute does not itself revise the definition; if the definition needs change, that is an explicit versioned revision. Historical applications, assessments and uses remain retained under the existing principles.

Concrete conflict-recognition, assessment, review and use contracts remain open. This refinement introduces no fixed vocabulary, confidence score or new requirement for rationale on every assignment, and makes no claim that definitions can never be ambiguous or revised.

## Recorded refinement: universal registration and the contract boundary

Everything digitally recordable must be registrable, directly or through a referencing registration, without prior content understanding, a prescribed source class or a fixed use. This includes contracts as well as sources, descriptions and generated context. The design test is whether the general registration foundation supports that breadth. Registrability does not grant source access or authorize intake. Preserving a reference alone does not establish preservation, integrity or continued availability of its target; reference guarantees remain open. See [P34](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p34-universal-registrability-without-prior-understanding).

Storage registers and preserves information, including properties used by an operational contract layer to assess and enforce whether comparisons may take place. Those properties are not predetermined to be labels. Computation, applicability assessment and contextual interpretation are separate from recording their inputs or results. A stored contract has identity, origin and versions, but storage alone neither activates it nor establishes authority or enforcement. See [P35](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p35-storage-and-operational-contract-responsibilities-are-distinct).

This responsibility boundary is not a physical deployment choice and does not weaken access enforcement at stored information under P30. F07's detailed processing-contract discussion remains open; no new comparison semantics are adopted here.

## Recorded refinement: enduring identity, historical records and current views

An enduring identity can connect sources without freezing its entire profile. Historical statements, results and actions retain the particular descriptions or values actually used where relevant; later changes do not silently retarget those references, rewrite sources or invalidate earlier descriptions. Current views may use newer knowledge without rewriting history. A new action selects applicable information under its operational contract, not merely the latest timestamp, and records the actual selection or use where relevant. Registration time remains distinct from substantive validity. See [P36](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p36-enduring-identity-historical-records-and-current-views-are-distinct).

F04, F06, F08, F12 and S03 reflect this distinction. Recorded combined states retain their participating versions while unchanged components can be reused; full copies and cascading revisions are not required. Concrete reference and version boundaries, correction, selection and reconstruction mechanisms remain open. No event-sourcing, temporal-storage or hash implementation is adopted.

## Recorded refinement: versioned metadata and narrative memory

Version history is the foundation. Validity, receipt and registration moments can be versioned as one coherent metadata set while remaining distinguishable observations or claims; unknown or conditional applicability is not forced into two timestamps. Grouping and reconstruction mechanisms remain open. See [P37](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p37-version-history-supports-metadata-sets-and-narrative-context).

Versioned wiki contexts can retain richer temporal and contextual meaning than compact fields. Labels provide explicit associations, with any recorded confidence or strength belonging to a particular assignment or assessment rather than the shared definition. Scores do not establish probability or truth, and derived repetition is not independent support. Context remains an attributable stored source with correction history; later extraction follows P26–P28. The brain analogy adopts no neural architecture, mandatory scoring scheme, event sourcing or bitemporal-storage mechanism.

## Recorded refinement: lifecycle metadata and the staging view

A candidate remains the same source registration through admission and subsequent existence. Lifecycle metadata stays with it and uses the general version-history framework; operational descriptions remain distinct from content labels, substantive validity and source-content versions. Staging selects registrations needing work rather than establishing a second source authority. Leaving that view preserves the source and history, and later work can bring it back. See [P38](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#p38-lifecycle-metadata-stays-with-the-source-registration).

Lifecycle contracts define meaning and admissible transition conditions; recording them does not activate or enforce them. Lifecycle state alone proves neither capture completeness nor availability. Access expiry, applicability, retention and requested versus confirmed destruction remain distinguishable; owner authority under P16 is unchanged. Concrete descriptions, transition evidence and view-membership rules remain open, without adopting a state catalog, schema or technology.

## Using the map

Choose the next discussion aspect together; no next priority has been accepted. For each aspect, use the same four questions:

1. What is its responsibility?
2. What should it guarantee, and where are the limits?
3. Which essential distinctions must it preserve?
4. How does it connect to the rest of the digital brain?

Record questions and proposals separately from accepted decisions. This working map may evolve as discussion develops; accepted changes belong in the maintained ADR with their history. It does not authorize implementation or live source capture.

## Details still open

Enduring identity, historical records and current views are distinguished; concrete reference/version boundaries, applicable-description selection for new uses, correction rules and reconstruction remain open. Coherent metadata grouping, confidence/strength representation and interpretation, and extraction from narrative context remain open. Same-registration lifecycle metadata, admissible transitions and their evidence, and staging-view membership and re-entry require concrete contracts. Technical choices, specific properties and base sets, meanings and vocabularies, concrete contract structures and evolution rules, precise intake scope criteria and admission validation, and process design remain open. Universal registrability still needs to be assessed against the general registration design, including contracts and direct versus referencing registration guarantees. Storing contract artifacts remains separate from their activation, authority, applicability and operational enforcement; concrete roles and source-property forms remain open. Handling undetermined scope membership, future-only intake versus historical backfill on widening, intake timing and progress mechanisms, coverage assessment, and outbound behavior also remain open. The accepted recognition questions still need concrete source-specific contracts: evidence for equality and lineage, identifier authority and reset handling, package contents, completion guarantees, conflict and unknown handling. Description contract questions are accepted across the three domains; concrete description types and base sets, admissible forms, interpretation limits and comparability requirements remain to be defined. Validity, replacement, restriction and withdrawal still require concrete evidence and rules for their scope and effective conditions. Retrieval and comparison guarantees, historical-result reconstruction, label origin and assignment contracts, candidate assessment and acceptance, and concrete applicability or review behavior also remain open. Origin is required for newly stored labels; a separate rationale for every assignment is not universally required. Produced context must be stored, while artifact forms, storage realization and contextualization methods remain undecided. Concrete retrieval response contracts remain open, without an assumed bundle of all descriptions. Global owner-access protection, bounded disclosure/access routes, their permitted information selections and operations, delegated-authority scope, derivative handling, and effective/release boundaries under concurrent retrieval need definition. Per-entity multi-party rights are not a planned phase; they would require explicit owner reconsideration. The earlier RLS exploration is historical and selects no technology. Concrete completeness and coverage evidence, unknown-coverage handling, partial-result requirements, recipient decision boundaries, thresholds, stop or continuation rules and presentation remain open. Conflict recognition between source-related applications or statements, assessment and review of uncertain applications, and use-specific selection contracts need further discussion; an application dispute does not itself change its reusable definition. Access durations, dependency re-evaluation, recovery limits and destruction mechanics also require further discussion. The [open design details in ADR 0001](decisions/0001-digital-brain-foundational-principles.md#open-design-details) remain the accepted record of these boundaries.
